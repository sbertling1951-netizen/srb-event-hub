# Progressive Person Lifecycle and Identity Coalescence Architecture

**Status:** Proposed architecture guidance

**Date:** August 2, 2026

## Purpose

This document defines the conceptual lifecycle through which EpicentraX
stewards a Person's continuity over time. It interprets Progressive Identity
Stewardship alongside the Constitution, Tenant architecture, Workspace
Resolution, and Person–Tenant Relationship architecture.

It does not alter the Constitution or any ADR. It introduces no technical
design, persistence model, interface, or operational procedure.

## Enduring position

Every real person has one enduring identity. EpicentraX does not create a
separate identity merely because a person participates through a different
Tenant, Event, relationship, account, or moment in time.

Every Person is equally a Person. What may change over time is not the
Person's standing or reality, but the platform's confidence in reconnecting
that Person's lifetime continuity. Identity confidence belongs to evidence and
to governed conclusions drawn from it; it does not belong to the Person.

At the same time, EpicentraX must not require complete lifetime identity
certainty before preserving a relationship or experience. A Tenant may
identify someone it wishes to serve. The platform preserves that intended
relationship and the experience it creates without claiming that the intended
individual is already known to be any particular existing Person.

Participation proceeds. Identity attribution fails closed when governed
evidence is ambiguous or conflicting.

The distinction is essential: preserving an experience for an independently
represented Person is not an assertion that the platform has established that
Person's continuity with any other representation.

## The lifecycle

### 1. Tenant intention

A lifecycle may begin when a Tenant identifies an individual with whom it
wishes to establish a relationship. That intention may arise through an
invitation, registration, enrollment, appointment, import, referral, or other
lawful organizational interaction.

Tenant intention establishes neither canonical identity nor authority. It is
evidence of a Tenant's intended relationship in that Tenant's context. The
Tenant does not own the Person, their identity, or their lifetime history.

### 2. Relationship and participation

Relationship and participation have independent meaning.

A relationship concerns the enduring affiliation a Person may have with a
Tenant. Participation concerns a particular experience, Event, registration,
or activity. Either may begin before the platform can safely establish broader
identity continuity.

An incomplete identity understanding must not erase, delay, or diminish a
valid relationship or experience. Each is preserved in its own proper context
and retains the circumstances in which it began.

### 3. Independent Person representation

When the platform cannot safely attribute an intended relationship or
experience to an existing Person, it may preserve it through an independently
represented Person. This is a continuity-preserving act, not an automatic
claim about who that individual is in relation to other People.

The platform must not select a plausible Person merely because the selection
would be convenient. A matching name, contact detail, household context,
membership value, imported label, or historical record may be meaningful
evidence, but it is not conclusive when it is ambiguous, conflicting, or not
sufficiently governed.

Independent representation gives the present relationship and experience a
truthful home while leaving broader identity continuity open for later,
governed work.

### 4. Experience preservation

Every experience remains historically truthful from the moment it occurs.
Its Event, Tenant, relationship, activity, and operational context are
preserved without being flattened into generic identity history.

Later continuity work may reconnect experiences that belong to the same real
Person. It must not erase their origin, change the Tenant or Event that
created them, or grant one Tenant visibility into another Tenant's context.
Jointly Contextual History is preserved by connecting truthful contexts to a
Person's continuing story, not by copying or fragmenting that story.

### 5. Identity strengthening

Identity understanding may strengthen over time through governed evidence,
appropriate participation, and the Person's own actions. Strengthening may
confirm a relationship, clarify an account association, add context to an
earlier experience, or establish that previously separate representations
require careful review.

Evidence is preserved with its provenance and meaning. It is not silently
converted into certainty because it is old, convenient, supplied by a Tenant,
or repeated in several contexts. Evidence may support a conclusion while
remaining distinct from the conclusion itself.

### 6. Person-directed memory recovery

A Person may seek to reconnect experiences, relationships, memories, and
learning that belong to their own story. EpicentraX supports that work as a
Person–Platform partnership rather than as a Tenant claim over the Person's
history.

The Person's agency matters. Recovery should enable understanding and
continuity while respecting the context in which every experience was created,
the privacy of other people, and each Tenant's legitimate boundaries.

### 7. Governed identity coalescence

Identity coalescence is the governed determination that separately represented
history belongs to one enduring Person. It is not a registration prerequisite,
an invitation prerequisite, or an ordinary operational shortcut.

Coalescence requires sufficient governed evidence and appropriate authority.
Where evidence is ambiguous, disputed, incomplete, or conflicting, the
platform does not guess. It preserves the separate representations, the
evidence, and the opportunity for later clarification.

The objective is continuity, not data reduction. Coalescence reconnects a
Person's story without allowing a Tenant to redefine the Person, absorb other
Tenant history, or erase the provenance of an experience.

### 8. Correction and reversibility

Identity stewardship must remain correctable. New evidence, a Person's
clarification, a discovered error, or a changed understanding may require a
prior conclusion to be reviewed and corrected.

Correction preserves history of what was understood and why. It does not
silently rewrite the past, conceal prior uncertainty, or destroy the original
relationship and participation context. Reversibility is a duty of trust, not
a reason to treat identity decisions casually.

## Identity Reconnection Lifecycle

Participation may begin immediately when a valid relationship or experience
is present. Continuity may be strengthened throughout a Person's lifetime as
the Person and platform gain governed evidence together.

Reconnection is initiated with the Person, never imposed upon them. EpicentraX
may assist a Person in discovering prior experiences that may belong to their
story, while respecting the context, privacy, and provenance of each
experience.

Reconnection is governed, transparent, auditable, and correctable. Identity
attribution always remains fail-closed when evidence is ambiguous, disputed,
or conflicting. The platform does not guess which prior experience belongs to
which Person.

Preserving experiences and reconnecting experiences are complementary phases
of one lifecycle. Preservation ensures that present value is not lost while
continuity is incomplete. Reconnection helps a Person later understand and
strengthen the continuity of their own story.

## Relationship initiation and trust context

An invited participant represents an existing relationship intentionally
created by a Tenant. Self-registration represents an unsolicited request to
establish a new relationship. These are different trust situations.

Progressive Identity Stewardship permits different governed identity workflows
for those situations without weakening the Constitution. In either situation,
the platform distinguishes relationship intention from identity attribution,
and neither situation permits automatic attribution from ambiguous evidence.

## Platform-wide identity stewardship

Identity stewardship is performed for the Person as a whole, independent of
any single Tenant. Relationship stewardship remains Tenant-scoped. Identity
matching is therefore a governed platform responsibility rather than a Tenant
responsibility.

This distinction protects both continuity and autonomy: the platform can help
a Person reconnect their story without giving any Tenant ownership of the
Person or access beyond that Tenant's own relationship and experience context.

## Continuity across Tenants

A Person may have relationships and experiences across many Tenants while
remaining one Person. Each Tenant stewards its own relationship and
experiences; no Tenant owns the Person or gains unrestricted access to another
Tenant's information.

Cross-Tenant continuity belongs to the Person and the platform's governed
identity stewardship. It exists to help the Person retain a whole story, not
to create cross-Tenant surveillance, duplicate identities, or convenience
access to history.

Tenant identity remains distinct from Person identity. Tenant routing,
branding, labels, hostnames, membership conventions, and local operational
terms are never proof of a Person's identity or authority.

## Provenance and auditability

Every material identity conclusion, unresolved ambiguity, and correction must
remain understandable in context. Future architects must be able to answer:

1. What relationship or experience was being preserved?
2. Which Tenant and Event contexts applied?
3. What evidence was available at the time?
4. What did that evidence establish, and what did it not establish?
5. Why did the platform attribute, defer, reconnect, correct, or preserve
   separate continuity?

Provenance protects both continuity and trust. It allows later review without
requiring the platform to pretend that past uncertainty never existed.

## Attribution, authority, and workspace

Identity, participation, relationship, authority, and workspace remain
separate concepts.

- **Identity** answers who the Person is, and whether continuity with another
  Person has been governedly established.
- **Participation** preserves a Person's connection to a specific experience.
- **Relationship** records how a Person is affiliated with a Tenant.
- **Authority** is derived, scoped, governed, and auditable; it is never
  inferred from participation, a relationship, a hostname, browser state, or
  incomplete identity evidence.
- **Workspace** consumes authoritative context. It does not establish
  identity, create a relationship, infer authority, or decide coalescence.

An unresolved continuity question does not invalidate a truthful
participation record. Conversely, participation and relationship never by
themselves establish operational authority. Missing or ambiguous identity,
Tenant, Event, relationship, assignment, or authority context remains
fail-closed wherever that context is required for action.

## Architectural invariants

1. Every real Person has one enduring identity.
2. Participation is not withheld solely because lifetime identity continuity
   remains unresolved.
3. Ambiguous or conflicting evidence never authorizes automatic attribution.
4. A Tenant may establish and preserve its relationship and experiences
   without owning or redefining the Person.
5. Every experience retains its original Tenant, Event, relationship, and
   operational provenance.
6. Identity coalescence is governed, transparent, auditable, and correctable.
7. Coalescence reconnects history; it does not erase contextual boundaries or
   create authority.
8. Authentication, authorization, and governed identity claims remain
   protected by their own constitutional rules.
9. Workspace resolution consumes established context and never becomes a
   second source of identity or authority.
10. Convenience, legacy labels, repeated appearances, and browser state never
    substitute for governed identity attribution.

## Guidance for future architectural decisions

Future architecture should preserve both halves of this principle:

1. Does the decision preserve a valid relationship or experience without
   creating an unnecessary identity barrier?
2. Does it keep identity attribution governed, transparent, auditable, and
   correctable?
3. Does it preserve the Person's opportunity to reconnect their own story?
4. Does it retain the original Tenant and Event context of each experience?
5. Does it avoid turning participation, relationship, or incomplete evidence
   into authority?

These questions supplement, and do not replace, the Constitution's guiding
questions or the governing ADRs.
